The shift is bigger than another e-invoicing mandate.

Across the GCC, three things are converging at once:

  • 1E-invoicing rollouts are maturing beyond pilot phases,
  • 2VAT oversight is becoming data-driven rather than filing-driven,
  • 3and Pillar Two is reshaping how large multinational groups report and structure tax.

Individually, each is a compliance project. Together, they're a test of whether your tax function can produce clean, connected, audit-ready data on demand — not once a quarter, but continuously.

Most enterprises are still organised around the old model: compliance as a periodic exercise, owned by tax, executed close to deadlines. That model doesn't hold up against transaction-level visibility. This session is about what replaces it.

Why This Session, Now

The mandates rolling out across the GCC aren't isolated events; they're markers of a longer shift toward continuous, data-driven tax administration. Businesses that treat each mandate as a standalone project end up rebuilding the same infrastructure market by market. Businesses that treat this as an infrastructure decision get ahead of every mandate that follows.

This session isn't a walkthrough of any single product or platform. It's a working conversation on what tax leaders should be building toward, drawn from the vantage point of someone managing this at group level for a multinational with GCC-wide exposure.
