SII (Immediate Supply of Information)

  • AEAT
  • Ministry of Finance

In force since 1 July 2017. Applies primarily to monthly VAT taxpayers, including large businesses, IVA groups and REDEME taxpayers, as well as voluntary participants.

Submit electronic VAT ledger records generally within four days of the transaction. SII does not require transmission of the complete invoice.

Crea y Crece B2B e-invoicing

  • Ministry of Economy
  • Commerce and Enterprise
  • Ministry of Finance
  • AEAT

Real Decreto 238/2026 entered into force on 20 April 2026. Obligations apply 12 months after the final Ministerial Order for businesses with turnover above €8 million, and 24 months after for other covered businesses and professionals.

  • Exchange structured electronic invoices for domestic B2B transactions
  • Support platform interoperability
  • Transmit invoice-status and payment information
  • Send a faithful electronic copy to the AEAT public solution

SIF / RRSIF — VERI*FACTU

  • AEAT
  • Ministry of Finance

Mandatory from 1 January 2027 for corporate income-tax taxpayers in scope and 1 July 2027 for other covered taxpayers. SII taxpayers are generally outside the RRSIF scope for covered invoicing activities.

Invoicing software must generate secure, traceable and unalterable billing records. VERIFACTU is the mode that sends these records to AEAT when invoices are issued.

B2G e-invoicing — Facturae / FACe

  • Public administrations
  • FACe is operated for the General State Administration

Mandatory for specified categories of public-sector suppliers since 15 January 2015.

Submit structured electronic invoices to the relevant public-sector entry point. Facturae is the established Spanish B2G format, while FACe is the General State Administration's invoice-entry platform.

TicketBAI and Batuz

  • Provincial Treasuries of Bizkaia
  • Gipuzkoa and Álava
  • together with the Basque Government

Already implemented under separate foral legislation and territory-specific rules. In Bizkaia, TicketBAI forms part of Batuz.

Use compliant invoicing software to create, sign and transmit prescribed invoice records.

Everything you need to know about global e-invoicing mandates, compliance, and the ClearTax platform.

Global e-invoicing exchanges invoices in structured, machine-readable formats (XML, UBL) through government-designated systems or regulated networks like Peppol - replacing paper and PDF invoices with a fully digital, auditable process.

Countries enforce one of three models:

  • Clearance: Tax authority approves the invoice before it reaches the buyer (e.g., Saudi Arabia, India, Mexico).
  • Post-audit / Reporting: Invoices are submitted to the authority after issuance (e.g., Germany, France).
  • Exchange: Structured invoices are transmitted through a regulated network like Peppol (e.g., Belgium, Croatia).

Over 100 countries have active or upcoming mandates, each with different formats, timelines, and penalties. Country-by-country management creates compliance gaps and duplicated costs.

A global solution provides: one integration for all mandates, centralised visibility across entities, and auto-updates that adapt without ERP rework.

A global e-invoicing network routes structured invoices between businesses and tax authorities. The most widely adopted is Peppol. National portals include India's IRP, Saudi Arabia's Fatoora, and Malaysia's MyInvois.

Businesses connect through a certified Access Point (like ClearTax), which handles formatting, validation, signing, and transmission to the correct authority or buyer.

Peppol (Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine) is a not-for-profit international network for exchanging structured business documents. Governed by OpenPeppol, it uses a four-corner model where sender and receiver each connect through their own certified Access Point.

As of 2026, 46+ countries are OpenPeppol members with 1.4 million registered participants, including:

  • Mandatory B2B/B2G: Belgium, Norway, Australia, Singapore, Japan, Croatia.
  • Phased mandates: Germany, France, Netherlands, Denmark, UAE, New Zealand.
  • Voluntary/pilot: UK, US, Canada, India, South Africa.
  • Fragmented regulations: 100+ countries with different formats, models, and deadlines.
  • Frequent changes: Mandates update regularly; grace periods expire with short notice.
  • Complex integrations: Connecting legacy systems to multiple portals is costly without pre-built connectors.
  • Data precision: Structured formats require exact data; errors cause immediate rejection.
  • Multi-entity compliance: Subsidiaries face different rules and staggered go-live dates.
  • Data residency: Local laws often prevent invoice data crossing borders.

Financial fines

  • Italy: EUR 250 to EUR 2,000 per invoice; up to 180% of VAT for format violations.
  • France: EUR 50 per invoice, capped at EUR 15,000/year.
  • Germany: Up to EUR 5,000 per offence from 2027.
  • Poland: Up to 100% of the invoice VAT amount from January 2027.
  • Belgium: EUR 1,500 to EUR 5,000 for repeated non-compliance.
  • UAE: AED 5,000/month for non-implementation; AED 100 per invoice.

Denied tax deductions

  • India: No GST input tax credit without a valid IRN.
  • Saudi Arabia: ZATCA can deny VAT credit on non-integrated invoices.

Operational blocks

  • Brazil and Mexico: Uncleared invoices have no legal standing; revenue cannot be recognised.
  • Egypt: Non-compliance can lead to criminal liability for executives.
