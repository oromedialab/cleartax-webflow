Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about global e-invoicing mandates, compliance and the ClearTax platform.

Global e-invoicing exchanges invoices in structured, machine-readable formats such as XML or UBL, through government-designated systems or regulated networks like Peppol — replacing paper and PDF invoices with a fully digital, auditable process.

Countries enforce one of three models:

  • Clearance — the tax authority approves the invoice before it reaches the buyer (Saudi Arabia, India, Mexico).
  • Post-audit / reporting — invoices are submitted to the authority after issuance (Germany, France).
  • Exchange — structured invoices travel through a regulated network like Peppol (Belgium, Croatia).

Over 100 countries have active or upcoming mandates, each with different formats, timelines and penalties. Managing them country by country creates compliance gaps and duplicated cost.

A global solution gives you one integration for all mandates, centralised visibility across entities, and auto-updates that adapt without ERP rework.

A global e-invoicing network routes structured invoices between businesses and tax authorities. The most widely adopted is Peppol. National portals include India's IRP, Saudi Arabia's Fatoora and Malaysia's MyInvois.

Businesses connect through a certified Access Point such as ClearTax, which handles formatting, validation, signing and transmission to the correct authority or buyer.

Peppol (Pan-European Public Procurement OnLine) is a not-for-profit international network for exchanging structured business documents. Governed by OpenPeppol, it uses a four-corner model in which sender and receiver each connect through their own certified Access Point.

As of 2026, 46+ countries are OpenPeppol members with 1.4 million registered participants:

  • Mandatory B2B/B2G — Belgium, Norway, Australia, Singapore, Japan, Croatia.
  • Phased mandates — Germany, France, Netherlands, Denmark, UAE, New Zealand.
  • Voluntary or pilot — UK, US, Canada, India, South Africa.
  • Fragmented regulations — 100+ countries with different formats, models and deadlines.
  • Frequent changes — mandates update regularly and grace periods expire at short notice.
  • Complex integrations — connecting legacy systems to multiple portals is costly without pre-built connectors.
  • Data precision — structured formats demand exact data; errors cause immediate rejection.
  • Multi-entity compliance — subsidiaries face different rules and staggered go-live dates.
  • Data residency — local law often prevents invoice data crossing borders.
